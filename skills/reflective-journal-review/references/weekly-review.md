# Seven-day review workflow

## Purpose

Analyze the seven calendar days ending on an inclusive end date. Emphasize developments across days rather than concatenating seven daily summaries.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the end date and calculate the start date as six days earlier.
2. Select existing, non-empty raw entries in that range and note material source gaps when relevant.
3. Build a dated evidence map of events, stated reactions, ideas, decisions, and actions.
4. Compare days to find recurrence, escalation, reduction, contradiction, and explicit change.
5. Track every actionable idea from its first observed date through the latest supported state.
6. Draft the review and verify that cross-day claims rely on more than one dated observation unless explicitly stated by the writer.

## Output sections

Use only sections supported by the source, in this order:

1. **Seven-day overview** — the period's central arc.
2. **Cross-day themes** — important themes supported across dates.
3. **Recurring concerns** — repeated subjects, people, questions, or emotional cues without diagnosing them.
4. **Evolution of ideas** — dated changes in judgments, needs, priorities, or plans.
5. **Turning points** — events or realizations followed by a supported change.
6. **Unfinished ideas and first observed dates** — new, continuing, progress reported, or status unknown.
7. **Reported progress** — only concrete progress or completion recorded in the source.
8. **Next items to track** — evidence-based open loops, not invented obligations.
9. **Overall seven-day thread** — a concise synthesis of relationships among the supported patterns.

Do not infer that a missing later mention means completion. If the entire period lacks substantive entries, report that there is nothing to review and stop before saving.
