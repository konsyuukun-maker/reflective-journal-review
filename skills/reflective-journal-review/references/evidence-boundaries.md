# Evidence boundaries

## Evidence levels

Keep these categories distinct in reasoning and wording:

- **Recorded fact:** an event, action, decision, or statement directly present in the source.
- **Writer's view:** a feeling, judgment, preference, interpretation, or intention explicitly attributed to the writer.
- **Analytical observation:** a pattern inferred from multiple pieces of evidence. Use calibrated wording such as “appears,” “may,” or “the entries suggest.”
- **Optional suggestion:** a possible next step. Keep it separate from the review and include it only when requested or when the review explicitly calls for follow-up prompts.

## Prohibited leaps

- Do not infer a diagnosis, personality trait, hidden motive, or emotional state that the writer did not express.
- Do not convert an intention into an action, or an action into a completed outcome, without evidence.
- Do not treat silence on a later date as cancellation, completion, or loss of interest.
- Do not resolve contradictions on the writer's behalf. Describe the tension and its dates.
- Do not use confident causal language when the source supports only correlation or sequence.

## Tracking status

Use only these evidence-based states when tracking ideas or actions:

- **New:** first appears in the current period.
- **Continuing:** appeared earlier and is mentioned again without completion evidence.
- **Progress reported:** the source records a concrete step but not final completion.
- **Completed:** the source explicitly records completion or an unambiguous completed result.
- **Status unknown:** the idea appears, but later entries provide no reliable update.

Include the first observed date when available. Never upgrade a status based solely on elapsed time.
